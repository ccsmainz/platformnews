Demographics

Gender proportions

Across short video posts published by news outlets globally, women were visibly present in an average of 34% of the content. We observed differences based on the platform, outlet type, and country of origin. Looking at platforms, YouTube Shorts featured women significantly less often, at 29% of posts, compared to TikTok, which had women visible in 35% of posts, and Instagram, with 34%. Regarding outlet types, digital-native outlets and public broadcasters generally showed a higher share of visible women, at 36% and 37% respectively, while private broadcasters featured women in 32% of their posts, a significantly lower share than digital outlets. Finally, the visibility of women varied considerably by country, ranging from just 25% in South Korea to a high of 46% in Norway.

Average person age

The overall average age of persons featured in short video posts by news outlets was approximately 34 years. We observed a statistically significant difference across platforms, with YouTube Shorts featuring individuals aged 35.1 years on average, which was older than those featured on TikTok at an average of 33.4 years; Instagram posts showed an average age of 34.1 years. However, differences across outlet types were small and not statistically significant, with digital outlets featuring individuals aged 33.2 years on average, and print, public, and private broadcasters showing similar averages ranging from 33.7 to 34.4 years. We found notable variation across countries, with the average age of featured individuals ranging from a low of 29.5 years in Sweden to a high of 38.3 years in Poland.

Age diversity

We observed that the overall average age diversity, measured as the interquartile range of visible people’s ages in news outlets’ short video posts, was 13.8. When examining differences by platform, age diversity was similar across TikTok (13.4), Instagram (14.1), and YouTube Shorts (13.9), with no statistically significant differences among them. However, we identified a significant difference based on outlet type, with private broadcasters exhibiting higher age diversity at 14.2 compared to digital-only outlets, which showed 12.7. Differences among print, public, and private broadcasters were small and not statistically significant. Age diversity also varied substantially by country, ranging from a low of 11.4 in the Philippines to highs such as 16.3 in Poland, 16.2 in Hungary, and 16.2 in the United Kingdom.

Content

For topical diversity, we estimated the effective number of topics per outlet-platform combination, based on our automatic topic classification with 19 news categories.

Topic diversity

Across all news outlets and platforms, we observed an average topic diversity of 6.6 effective topics covered in short video posts. When examining platform differences, we found that videos on YouTube Shorts had the lowest topic diversity, averaging 5.5 topics, which was significantly lower than TikTok’s 6.9 topics. Instagram videos showed slightly higher diversity than TikTok, with an average of 7.2 topics. Regarding outlet type, private broadcasters averaged 7.2 topics and public broadcasters 7.4, both demonstrating significantly higher topic diversity compared to digital outlets, which averaged 6.1 topics, and print outlets, with 6.0 topics. We also identified substantial variations in topic diversity across countries, with some nations like Singapore (8.1) and Finland (8.0) exhibiting high diversity, while others such as Turkey (5.0) and Hungary (5.3) showed considerably lower levels of topic diversity.

Measures

We measured demographic diversity using the FairFace classifier (Kärkkäinen and Joo 2019), which classifies apparent gender, age group and ethnicity.

Topic diversity was estimated by computing the effective number of topics, which accounts for number of topics coded per outlet and their frequency distribution, see Laakso and Taagepera (1979) for a similar measure.
