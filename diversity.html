Demographics

Gender proportions

We found that the overall average share of visible women per post was 34%. When examining differences across platforms, we observed a statistically significant disparity: posts on YouTube Shorts featured a lower share of visible women, averaging 29%, compared to TikTok at 36% and Instagram at 35%, with differences between TikTok and Instagram being small and non-significant. Regarding outlet types, we saw meaningful differences, with digital outlets and public broadcasters both featuring women in 37% of their posts. In contrast, print outlets averaged 34% visible women, while private broadcasters showed the lowest share at 32%; these differences were statistically significant when compared to digital outlets. Finally, the share of visible women varied substantially by country, ranging from a low of 24% in South Korea to a high of 45% in Norway.

Average person age

We found that the average age of persons featured in short video posts was 34.1 years. This average varied significantly by platform and outlet type. On platforms, YouTube Shorts featured significantly older individuals at 35.2 years compared to TikTok’s 33.6 years, while Instagram posts featured individuals around 34.2 years. Regarding outlet type, digital outlets featured the youngest persons, averaging 33.3 years. In contrast, print, private broadcaster, and public broadcaster outlets all featured significantly older individuals, with average ages ranging from 34.0 to 34.6 years, approximately one year older than those in digital posts. Country also played a substantial role in the featured age; we observed a wide spread, from the youngest featured individuals in Sweden at 30.1 years to the oldest in Poland at 38.2 years, indicating noticeable geographical differences.

Content

For topical diversity, we estimated the effective number of topics per outlet-platform combination, based on our automatic topic classification with 19 news categories.

Topic diversity

Our analysis of short video posts revealed an average topic diversity of 5.4 effective topics per outlet. We found significant differences across platforms and outlet types. Looking at platforms, TikTok exhibited the highest topic diversity at 6.0, notably more diverse than Instagram (5.4) and YouTube Shorts (4.7), with YouTube Shorts showing the least variety. Regarding outlet types, public broadcasters covered the broadest range of topics, with an average diversity of 6.4, closely followed by private broadcasters at 5.8. Digital-only outlets and print outlets, on the other hand, displayed lower topic diversity, averaging 4.8 and 5.0 topics respectively, with both public and private broadcasters showing significantly greater diversity than digital outlets. Additionally, we observed considerable variation in topic diversity across countries, with some nations like Singapore (7.2) showing a much wider array of topics than others, such as Indonesia or Italy (around 3.4).

Measures

We measured demographic diversity using the FairFace classifier (Kärkkäinen and Joo 2019), which classifies apparent gender, age group and ethnicity.

Topic diversity was estimated by computing the effective number of topics, which accounts for number of topics coded per outlet and their frequency distribution, see Laakso and Taagepera (1979) for a similar measure.
