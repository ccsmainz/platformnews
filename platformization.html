Video and text features

Video format

We found that, on average, 62% of short video posts were native vertical videos. Examining differences by platform, we observed statistically significant variations: TikTok posts featured the highest share of native vertical videos at 67%, followed by Instagram at 59%, and YouTube Shorts at 55%. Outlet type also showed significant differences; public broadcasters posted the highest proportion of native vertical videos at 75%, while private broadcasters had the lowest at 49%. Digital and print outlets posted similar shares, both around 62%. Furthermore, we identified substantial variation across countries, with the share of native vertical videos ranging from a low of 34% in Hungary to a high of 75% in Finland, the Netherlands, Norway, and Sweden.

Emoji use

Across all short video posts analyzed, we found that 25% included emojis. We observed substantial and statistically significant differences across platforms: Instagram posts led with 41% containing emojis, followed by TikTok at 19%, and YouTube Shorts significantly lower at just 6%. When examining outlet types, digital outlets used emojis most frequently, appearing in 31% of their posts, which was significantly more often than print outlets, where only 22% of posts included emojis. Private and public broadcasters had similar usage rates, around 24%, showing small and non-significant differences from each other, but also less emoji use than digital outlets. We also found considerable variation by country, with emoji use ranging from very high in countries like the Czech Republic (63%), Spain (62%), and Slovakia (59%), to very low in Japan (2%), Canada (3%), Malaysia (3%), South Korea (3%), and the United States (3%).

Audience interactions

Addressing the audience

We found that, on average, 5% of short video posts directly addressed their audience. This share varied significantly by platform: Instagram posts most frequently addressed the audience at 7%, followed by TikTok at 5%, while YouTube Shorts had the lowest engagement in this regard, with only 3% of its posts. When examining outlet types, we observed that digital and print outlets had the highest shares, both at 7%, whereas private broadcasters showed a lower share of 4%, and public broadcasters were slightly higher at 5%. Furthermore, we saw statistically significant, varying differences across countries, with the share of posts addressing the audience ranging from as low as 4% in countries like Japan and South Korea to as high as 9% in Denmark.

Interaction calls

Across all analyzed short video posts, we found that, on average, 25% called for audience interactions such as liking, sharing, commenting, or visiting a website. However, this average masked substantial variations across platforms and outlet types. We observed significant differences by platform, with Instagram posts having the highest call for interaction at 49%, followed by TikTok at 16%, and YouTube Shorts significantly lower at just 7%. For outlet types, digital outlets prompted the most engagement, with 37% of their posts containing calls to action. This was notably higher than print outlets at 28%, public broadcasters at 26%, and private broadcasters, which had the lowest rate at 18%. We also found considerable variation across countries, with the share of posts requesting interaction ranging widely, from a low of 6% in South Korea to a high of 49% in Slovakia.

Measures

Video duration was provided as meta data from the platforms. Video format was coded using zero-shot classification with multimodal LLMs using video frames. Emoji use was measured using the emo_detect() function from the {emoji} package (Hvitfeldt 2024). Audience interactions were coded using zero-shot classification with multimodal LLMs using post text, transcript and video frames.

Coding instructions

Your task is to analyze the provided short video content based on the provided video frames, text and transcript. Do not infer beyond what you can directly observe in the text or images. Please evaluate the provided video content against each of the following 4 characteristics independently:

direct_audience_address: Code “True” if the narrator speaks directly to the viewer or the text addresses the audience using direct pronouns or imperatives (e.g., “Have you heard?”, “You need to see this,” or a person pointing at the camera). Code “False” if the content is purely observational or third-person reporting.

community_mentions: Code “True” if the video, caption, or transcript mentions specific audience members, usernames, or community feedback. This includes “Replying to (user?),” screenshots of comments, or phrases like “You guys have been asking” or “In the comments, people said.” Code “False” otherwise.

call_to_like: Code “True” if there is a verbal or visual prompt to “like,” “heart,” “save,” “bookmark,” or “upvote” (e.g., “Double tap if you agree,” “Save this for later”). Code “False” otherwise.

call_to_comment_discuss: Code “True” if there is a verbal or visual prompt to write a comment, answer a specific question, or engage in a discussion (e.g., “Tell us your thoughts,” “What do you think?”). Code “False” otherwise.

call_to_share: Code “True” if there is a verbal or visual prompt to share the video, send it to others, or repost it to a story/feed. Code “False” otherwise.

call_to_follow_subscribe: Code “True” if there is a verbal or visual prompt to follow the account, subscribe to the channel, or visit the profile/page for more content (e.g., “Hit the plus sign,” “Follow for more”). Code “False” otherwise.

call_to_website_app: Code “True” if there is a verbal or visual prompt to visit the outlet’s own digital products, such as a website for a full article or an app (e.g., “Link in bio,” “Read the full story on our site”). Code “False” otherwise.

call_to_traditional_media: Code “True” if there is a verbal or visual prompt to consume the outlet’s traditional media, such as watching a TV broadcast, listening to a radio show, or reading a print edition (e.g., “Watch the full report tonight on [Channel Name]”). Code “False” otherwise.

call_to_social_platform: Code “True” if there is a verbal or visual prompt to visit the outlet on a specifically mentioned social media platform (e.g., “Check our YouTube,” “Live updates on our Twitter”). Code “False” otherwise.
