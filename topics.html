Topics

Overview

We analyzed the topics of the posts by using zero-shot classification of the post title and transcript. Based on the topic list from the Electronic News Archive (ENA) of the University of Antwerp, we coded the presence of 19 from politics to sports, with a few platform-specific additions like user-generated performances, which includes typical dance and lip sync videos, or advertisements and promotions.

Perhaps surprisingly, “hard news” topics like politics and crime dominate, while typical platform content is relatively rarely published by news outlets.

In the subsections below, we provide detailed information about all coded news topics, along with comparisons by platform, media type and country.

Politics

On average, 38% of short video posts covered political topics. We found small, non-significant differences between platforms, with YouTube Shorts showing a slightly higher average (43%) compared to TikTok (37%). However, a notable contrast emerged between outlet types: Public broadcasters exhibited a 17 percentage point lower share of political content compared to digital-native outlets. Cross-nationally, we observed considerable variation, with countries such as Poland (59%) and Peru (54%) having the highest share of political content, while countries like Sweden (22%) and Switzerland (23%) had the lowest.

Law enforcement and crime

Our analysis of short videos revealed that, on average, 31% of posts covered law enforcement and crime. We observed minor, non-significant differences between TikTok (31%) and YouTube Shorts (33%). However, digital outlets showed significantly less coverage of law enforcement and crime (26%) compared to private (33%) and public broadcasters (32%). The share of posts about law enforcement and crime varied across countries, with Peru (50%) and Mexico (45%) showing the highest shares, while Italy (19%) and Spain (20%) showed the lowest.

War disasters accidents

Our analysis of short video posts reveals that, on average, 22% of content focused on war, disasters, and accidents. We found a slight but significant difference between platforms, with YouTube Shorts (24%) featuring slightly more of this content than TikTok (22%). Significant contrasts emerged among different outlet types, with public broadcasters showing a notably higher share (34%) of content related to war, disasters, and accidents compared to digital outlets (10%). Further, print outlets (18%) and private broadcasters (18%) also posted significantly more than digital outlets. There were also differences in the share of posts covering war disasters accidents by country.
